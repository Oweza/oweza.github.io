# REFLECTIONS

## Agile Team Charter Reflection

During my workplace-integrated learning at CAPACITI and Step Ahead, I developed an understanding of the importance of shared team objectives, professional communication, collaboration and individual accountability. At CAPACITI, I participated in daily stand-ups and group AI development projects. At Step Ahead, introductory meetings helped me understand my role, responsibilities and the company's working environment. I familiarised myself with Microsoft Teams, Azure DevOps and ClickUp to support communication and task management.

Working on InclusiVerse helped me appreciate the importance of shared values such as accessibility, security, quality and respect for user privacy. I learned that clear responsibilities and working agreements reduce misunderstandings and improve collaboration. Although I do not have evidence of a formal, co-created team charter, these experiences demonstrated its practical importance. In future projects, I would encourage the team to document and regularly review its working agreements as requirements and team needs change.

## User Stories and Tasks Reflection

During my placement, I learned to translate software requirements and user needs into manageable development tasks. I created Azure DevOps work items for website defects, accessibility improvements and proposed functionality for InclusiVerse. This helped me understand how user stories connect technical development to the needs of the end user.

For example, a user story for the employee invitation flow could be: **As an invited employee, I want to receive a clear message about the status of my invitation so that I know what action to take next.** This requirement can be broken down into tasks such as updating the invitation lookup API, identifying different invitation statuses, designing appropriate messages and validating authentication behaviour.

Another example from the SAS CV Formatter is: **As a recruiter, I want to compare a formatted CV with the original document so that I can verify the accuracy of candidate information.** The associated tasks included developing a side-by-side comparison interface, implementing text matching and colour-coded highlighting, and recording verification decisions.

These experiences taught me to focus on the value delivered to users rather than only the technical implementation. In future, I would define measurable acceptance criteria for each user story before development to make completion and testing easier to assess.

## Trello Reflection

Trello supports Agile project management through visual Kanban boards, where tasks can be represented as cards and moved between workflow stages such as To Do, In Progress, Review and Done. Although my documented workplace activities involved Azure DevOps and ClickUp rather than Trello, I gained experience with similar principles of organising tasks, documenting requirements and making development progress visible.

At Step Ahead, I created Azure DevOps work items for the InclusiVerse homepage revamp, recorded website defects and incorporated requirements from the homepage revamp checklist. I also attended an introductory session on ClickUp. These activities helped me understand how visual task management can improve accountability, communication and prioritisation.

I could apply the same principles in Trello by creating cards for user stories, adding task checklists and acceptance criteria, and updating their status as development progresses. In future, I would use a visual board consistently to identify blocked tasks and improve visibility of outstanding work.

## Product Backlog Reflection

My work on InclusiVerse and the SAS recruitment tool demonstrated the importance of maintaining a prioritised product backlog. The work included public website improvements, employee invitation functionality, accessibility enhancements, security fixes and recruitment automation features. Azure DevOps work items supported the documentation and organisation of requirements and defects.

I learned that tasks should be prioritised according to user value, business requirements, technical dependencies and risk. For example, the November security audit addressed sensitive API error exposure, company-level access restrictions and unauthorised access. These issues demonstrated why security vulnerabilities may need to take priority over visual improvements. Accessibility features such as screen reader support and Bigger Text also highlighted the importance of considering different user needs when prioritising work.

The PNet Search Optimizer and the CV comparison feature demonstrated how new functionality can address business needs and reduce manual recruitment work. In future, I would make prioritisation criteria explicit and review the backlog regularly with relevant stakeholders to ensure that it remains aligned with product goals.

## Sprint Backlog Reflection

Throughout my placement, I developed an understanding of how larger requirements can be divided into smaller, actionable tasks that support short-term development goals. At CAPACITI, I collaborated using Agile/Scrum practices, while at Step Ahead I used development branches, Azure DevOps work items and pull requests to support the implementation of software changes.

For example, the employee invitation flow involved updating the API, redesigning the invitation acceptance page, handling different invitation statuses and correcting Microsoft authentication behaviour. Dividing the work into smaller tasks helped me understand dependencies and manage development progress. The Easy Apply feature also demonstrated the importance of distinguishing completed interface work from outstanding functionality: the review page and consent controls were implemented, but application submission remained disabled as of 26 November 2026.

I learned that short-term plans must remain flexible when new defects, security concerns or changing requirements arise. Although I do not have evidence of a formal sprint backlog for every period at Step Ahead, I applied related principles through task breakdown and incremental development. In future, I would define clear sprint goals, estimate task effort and regularly update the sprint backlog to reflect progress and blockers.

## Agile Logbook Reflection

My Agile logbook records weekly workplace activities from July to November 2026, including completed tasks, planned activities and impediments. At CAPACITI, daily stand-up meetings supported team collaboration and progress tracking. My weekly workplace records at Step Ahead helped me document development activities, technical challenges and professional growth.

Examples of technical challenges included intermittent database connection failures affecting Microsoft sign-in, CV upload errors and accessibility issues involving duplicate screen reader announcements. Investigating these problems taught me the importance of identifying root causes, documenting findings and adapting development plans. I also learned that not every attempted solution should be recorded as a permanent achievement, as some authentication changes were subsequently removed.

Maintaining the logbook helped me reflect on my progress in software development, AI integration, accessibility, security, teamwork and time management. It also provided a record of my university study leave and subsequent return to workplace activities. The current logbook consolidates weekly records rather than providing a complete daily Scrum or retrospective record. In future, I would maintain daily entries, document sprint goals and record retrospective outcomes to improve accountability and support continuous improvement.
