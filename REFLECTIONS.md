# REFLECTIONS

## Agile Team Charter Reflection

During my workplace-integrated learning at CAPACITI and Step Ahead, I developed an understanding of teamwork, professional communication and individual accountability. At CAPACITI, I participated in daily stand-ups and collaborative AI projects, while at Step Ahead, introductory meetings helped me understand my responsibilities and the company's working environment. I familiarised myself with Microsoft Teams, Azure DevOps, ClickUp and SmarterEmail. Working with team members on InclusiVerse and the SAS tool taught me the importance of communicating progress, respecting responsibilities and protecting sensitive information. I learned that effective teamwork requires clear expectations, consistent communication and a shared understanding of project objectives.

## User Stories and Tasks Reflection

At Step Ahead, I learned to translate project requirements and identified problems into actionable work items using Azure DevOps. During the InclusiVerse homepage revamp, I documented website defects, proposed improvements and incorporated requirements from the homepage revamp checklist. Later tasks included improving employee invitations, accessibility, authentication and security. I also developed recruitment-related functionality for the SAS tool, including the PNet Search Optimizer and the Compare with Original feature for the CV Formatter. These activities helped me understand how technical tasks address real user needs. I learned to consider usability, accessibility, security and expected functionality when implementing changes, rather than focusing solely on completing the code.

## Product Backlog Reflection

Working with Azure DevOps introduced me to the importance of organising and prioritising project requirements. The InclusiVerse backlog included public-facing website improvements, employee invitation functionality, accessibility enhancements and security-related tasks. I learned that priorities can change when issues affecting users or data security are discovered. For example, sign-in failures, CV upload errors and company-level access vulnerabilities required attention alongside planned feature development. My experience taught me to distinguish essential fixes from visual enhancements and to consider their impact on users and business operations. Balancing workplace responsibilities with university examinations also reinforced the importance of realistic planning and communicating availability.

## Sprint Backlog Reflection

I developed my ability to break larger requirements into smaller, manageable development tasks. For the InclusiVerse homepage revamp, I worked on individual pages, shared styling and authentication interfaces before moving to employee invitation functionality. I used the development repository and pull requests to manage code changes and Azure DevOps work items to organise requirements. During the SAS tool development, I worked on the recruitment search optimizer and later on CV comparison functionality, including document matching, highlighting and verification history. Some tasks required investigation and revision, particularly authentication issues where proposed fixes were subsequently removed. This taught me to review progress regularly, adapt to changing requirements and recognise that a proposed solution is not necessarily a completed or permanent improvement.

## Agile Logbook Reflection

Maintaining my workplace logbook helped me document completed activities, identify challenges and plan subsequent work. At CAPACITI, daily stand-ups and Agile/Scrum collaboration supported my progress in AI learning, chatbot development and the Content Generator project. At Step Ahead, weekly records captured my contributions to InclusiVerse and the SAS tool from onboarding through development, accessibility improvements, security enhancements and recruitment automation. Recording challenges such as database connection interruptions, CV upload failures and accessibility defects helped me reflect on my problem-solving approach. The logbook also highlighted the importance of accurately recording unfinished work, such as the Easy Apply application interface, whose submission functionality was still pending at the end of the recorded period. Through this process, I improved my ability to communicate progress, evaluate my learning and maintain accountability for my workplace responsibilities.
